How to read this map

Isobars connect points with equal pressure. Lines close together = big pressure change over a short distance = strong wind.

Wind starts blowing from high toward low pressure, but Earth's rotation bends it: clockwise & outward around a High, counterclockwise & inward around a Low (Northern Hemisphere). Friction at the ground turns it about 30° toward the Low.

Highs: air sinks → clear, calm. Lows: air rises → clouds and precipitation.

Fronts are boundaries between air masses. The symbols point the way the front is moving. Cold fronts: sharp temperature drop, showers or storms, wind shift.

Jet stream: the fast river of air near 18,000 ft (500 mb). Surface Lows and fronts tend to form and travel along it, and the height lines show its troughs (dips) and ridges (bulges).

Precipitation shows how much falls in each 6-hour period. Compare it with the Lows and fronts.
