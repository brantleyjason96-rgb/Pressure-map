📖 How to read this map

Isobars connect points with equal pressure. Lines close together = big pressure change over a short distance = strong wind.

Wind blows from high toward low pressure, but Earth’s rotation bends it: in the Northern Hemisphere clockwise & outward around a High, counterclockwise & inward around a Low. In the Southern Hemisphere it is the opposite.

Highs: air sinks → clear, calm. Lows: air rises → clouds and precipitation.

Fronts are boundaries between air masses. The symbols point the way the front is moving.

Dotted trails show where each High and Low was over the last 48 hours. Click an H or L to see how fast and which way it is moving.

Jet stream: the fast river of air near 18,000 ft (500 mb) that steers Lows and fronts.

Click anywhere for the values at that spot, then choose it as your place.

Pressure, temperature, wind and rain are NOAA GFS model analyses. Fronts are forecasters’ hand analysis.

Make your own 3-day forecast

This map shows the weather up to now. It never shows the future. Your job is to study the evidence and predict the next 3 days for a place you choose.

  1. Explore. Slide the time bar through the last 48 hours. Watch Highs, Lows and fronts move. Click an H or L to see its speed and direction.
  2. My Place. Choose a place. See its normal weather and what has been happening there.
  3. Local. Check mountains, the coast and ocean currents. They change the weather in ways the big map can’t show.
  4. Notes. Press 📌 Add to notes whenever you find evidence. Use your notes to build your presentation.
  5. Save your work. Press 💾 My work to download a file. You can open it later, even on another computer.

💾 My work

Your place, notes and drawn fronts save automatically in this browser, so you can come back tomorrow. They are lost if you clear your browser’s cookies and site data, or use a different computer. Save a file now and then as a backup, and open it to continue anywhere.
