How to read this map

Isobars connect points with equal pressure. Lines close together = big pressure change over a short distance = strong wind.

Wind blows from high toward low pressure, but Earth’s rotation bends it: in the Northern Hemisphere clockwise & outward around a High, counterclockwise & inward around a Low. In the Southern Hemisphere it is the opposite.

Highs: air sinks → clear, calm. Lows: air rises → clouds and precipitation.

Fronts are boundaries between air masses. The symbols point the way the front is moving.

Dotted trails show where each High and Low was over the last 48 hours. Click an H or L to see how fast and which way it is moving.

Jet stream: the fast river of air near 18,000 ft (500 mb) that steers Lows and fronts.

Click anywhere for the values at that spot, then choose it as your place.

Pressure, temperature, wind and rain are NOAA GFS model analyses. Fronts are forecasters’ hand analysis.
